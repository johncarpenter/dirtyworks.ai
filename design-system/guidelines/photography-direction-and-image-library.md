## Purpose

Add real human presence to the website without falling into generic AI imagery or polished corporate-stock theatre. The photographs should make the managed service tangible: people reading, comparing, teaching, supporting, marking, checking, and resolving.

Photography is not customer proof. Until an image is an approved photograph of a real Dirtyworks.ai engagement, it must not be captioned or positioned in a way that implies the people shown are customers, employees, or partners.

## Recommended image strategy

Use three layers rather than forcing one source to do every job:

| Layer | Use | Strength | Constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Original generated editorial images | Prototype, early website, controlled industry scenes, exact crops | Specific to the brand; avoids overused stock; easy negative space | People/events are fictional; maintain provenance and never imply customer evidence |
| Licensed real stock | Training, teamwork, documents, and human-detail sections | Real people and real photography | Can feel generic or appear on other sites; model/property/licence terms must be retained |
| Commissioned local photography | Public hero, founder, team, Calgary/Alberta operations, future customer proof | Most credible and ownable | Requires production, releases, location, budget, and scheduling |

**Working recommendation:** use the generated set for the design prototype and private launch. For a durable public brand, replace at least the hero and About/founder photography with a small commissioned local shoot. Retain the generated energy-services and incident scenes only as clearly editorial brand imagery if they remain useful.

## Photography rules

### Show

- people absorbed in real work rather than looking at camera;
- experienced workers and mixed-age teams, not only young startup employees;
- hands marking, comparing, teaching, testing, documenting, or resolving;
- paper and digital tools in the same frame;
- real material: worn wood, paper, binders, wool, cotton, steel, cables, practical offices;
- direct flash or hard side light, subtle grain, and natural skin texture;
- Alberta through winter light, materials, modest offices, and industrial context—not landmarks or costumes;
- negative space that can accept a solid ink or bone copy capsule;
- a clear human relationship to the service claim near the image.

### Avoid

- people smiling or pointing at the camera;
- handshakes, high-fives, boardroom lineups, and staged applause;
- glowing brains, robots, holograms, code reflected in glasses, neon, purple gradients, and futuristic command centres;
- pristine glass technology campuses and identical young startup teams;
- fake PPE, hard hats worn unnecessarily indoors, unsafe industrial behaviour, oil derricks used as shorthand for Calgary;
- readable confidential documents, identifiable company systems, credentials, logos, or vendor UI;
- soft protection gradients over photography. Put text beside the image or on a solid square-cornered capsule;
- images presented as customer proof without a release, attribution/approval, and truthful caption.

### Hero alternatives

There are two valid homepage treatments:

1. **Photo-led:** full-bleed operations-leader image with a solid copy capsule on the left; the AI portfolio register becomes the next band. This creates immediate human presence.
2. **Type-led:** retain the oversized declaration/register hero and place the operations-leader image as the first full-width interruption immediately after it. This preserves the most distinctive typographic opening.

The sponsor can choose. The photo-led route is warmer; the type-led route is more ownable and brand-forward.

### Licence note

Pexels currently states that its photos can be used for free, attribution is not required, modification is allowed, and website/marketing use is permitted. Its restrictions include not implying that identifiable people endorse the product, not placing people in a bad/offensive light, not reselling an unaltered image, and not using an image as a trademark ([Pexels licence](https://www.pexels.com/license/), reviewed 2026-08-25).

This is a practical source note, not legal advice. Capture the applicable licence and asset page at download because terms and asset availability can change.

## Treatment and cropping rules

- Produce `avif` and `webp` derivatives from an approved source; preserve the original PNG/JPEG separately.
- Keep meaningful focal content intact at 16:9, 4:3, and mobile 4:5 crops.
- Do not use blur, frosted glass, or gradient scrims over a face.
- Copy over a photo must sit in a solid bone or ink rectangle with square corners and sufficient contrast.
- Texture treatments may be applied to a duplicate: monochrome halftone, ink duotone, or restrained carbon transfer. Keep an untreated source.
- Do not recolour skin with orange, acid, or blueprint overlays. Brand colour can enter through borders, labels, crops, or nearby fields.
- Alt text should describe the visible work, not the marketing claim. Example: `An operations manager compares an annotated procedure with a laptop at a wooden desk.`
- Avoid repeating the same people as if they appear in multiple customer organizations.
